build_doc_ issue codes

What each issue in a department's GRs means, by the step of the flow that finds it, in the order the steps run. A problem found early (text read wrongly) often causes the ones after it (a field or a section not found), so look at the earlier steps' issues first. info issues are notes (nothing may be wrong); warnings usually point at something to fix.

text: reading the PDF's text (pdf_text)

The page, or some words on it, were read by OCR (Google Cloud Vision) instead of from the PDF's text layer.

Why: the page is a scan (no text layer), its text layer is hidden under the page's image, or the text layer uses an old (non-Unicode) Marathi font that cannot be converted. A note, not a fault: OCR is usually good, but it can misread small print, stamps and tables.

What to do: nothing, unless the words look wrong; then correct them at the Words level.

Some characters of an old (non-Unicode) Marathi font could not be converted to Unicode.

Why: the font's converter has no mapping for a character (a rare conjunct, a symbol). The word around it may be missing a letter or show a stray character.

What to do: look at the words named in the issue; correct them at the Words level if they read wrongly.

A font on the page looks like an old (non-Unicode) Marathi font, and there is no converter for it.

Why: a font not seen before. Its words are read by OCR instead (see core:text_by_ocr), so the text is usually fine; many GRs with the same font are worth a converter.

What to do: usually nothing; check a few words.

The page's text layer lost its reph (the र्‍ that sits on top of a letter, as in "कार्य"), so words come out wrong.

Why: a common fault of some PDF makers. The page is read by OCR instead, so the text is usually right.

What to do: check a few words with a reph; correct at the Words level if needed.

text_check: checking the Marathi text (deva_check)

Words on the page that cannot be correct Marathi: a vowel sign at the start of a word, two vowel signs in a row, another script's letter (Bengali "ী" for ी), a Latin letter inside a Marathi word, an old font's mark (¨), or a blank or unknown character (a Symbol-font bullet shown as a box).

Why: the text layer or the OCR decoded the font wrongly. The issue lists the words and the reason for each.

What to do: correct the words at the Words level. Many on one page usually means the whole page was decoded wrongly: say so in a note rather than fixing word by word.

table_check: checking the tables (pdf_tables)

A line of text reaches past the edges of its table cell.

Why: the table's grid was found slightly wrong (a column too narrow), or the text overflows its cell in the PDF itself. Text may be in the wrong cell or column.

What to do: look at the table; if a cell's text belongs to its neighbour, fix it at the Tables level.

A column of the table is empty in every row below its header.

Why: often fine (a "remarks" column left blank); sometimes a sign that the grid has an extra column line, and the text went into the next column.

What to do: check that the other columns' text is in the right place.

A row labelled total (एकूण) is not the sum of the rows above it.

Why: a number misread (OCR, a spilled cell), a row missed, or the printed total is itself wrong.

What to do: compare the numbers with the PDF; correct a misread number at the Words level. A total wrong in the PDF itself is left as printed.

A scanned page has ruled lines that could not be made into one table, so its table is kept as plain text.

Why: the scan's lines are broken, skewed or faint. The text is all there, but not in rows and columns.

What to do: nothing for the text; the table's structure is lost on this page.

role_check: checking the fields and sections (patterns)

A field every GR should have was not found: the subject, the order kind (शासन निर्णय, परिपत्रक…), the order number, the issue date, the GR code, or the signature.

Why: it is not printed (older GRs often have no GR code or order number), it is printed in a way the patterns do not know, or the text around it was read wrongly. The issue says which field.

What to do: if the field is printed, select its words and give it the field (Fields level; the Missing fields queue takes the order kind and number one GR after another: click the words, Enter). If it is not printed, mark it so (x in the queue). The portal's date and GR code fill the export where none is printed.

A field was found but its value could not be read: a date that is not a date, an order number with no number.

Why: unusual writing ("दिनांक : माहे जून"), or a misread digit.

What to do: check the field's words; correct a misread word, or the field's value.

The issue date is after the date in the GR code. The code carries the day the GR was put on the portal, and a GR is issued before it is put up, not after.

Why: a misread date (a 2 read as 7), the wrong date taken as the issue date (a reference's date), or the code misread.

What to do: check which of the two is wrong against the PDF and correct it.

A page's running header (the line at the top of later pages, with the order number) does not carry the order number found on the first page.

Why: the order number was misread on one of them, or the wrong number was taken as the order number.

What to do: compare the two with the PDF; correct the one that is wrong.

Numbered paragraphs or list items skip or repeat a number (1, 2, 4… or 1, 2, 2…).

Why: a paragraph's number misread or not found (it starts a line inside a block), or the GR itself numbers them so.

What to do: look at the paragraphs around it; a paragraph run into the one before should be split (Blocks level).

A field's text is not what such a field looks like (an order number with no digits).

Why: the wrong words taken as the field.

What to do: select the right words for the field, or remove it.

Blocks of text that belong to no section (not the subject, references, preamble, decision, signature, copy list or an annexure).

Why: a heading not recognised, or text between sections that the patterns do not place (a note, a stamp).

What to do: give the blocks their section, if they have one.

Blocks given to two sections at once.

Why: two headings' patterns both matched.

What to do: keep the right section for them.

A section was found from where it sits, not from its heading (the decision with no "शासन निर्णय" heading, the copy list without "प्रत").

Why: many GRs leave a heading out. A note: the section is usually right.

What to do: check that the section starts and ends at the right blocks.

block_check: blocks to look at before their text is trusted (block_check)

A block of running text ends mid-sentence and the next block goes on with it: one sentence cut in two by the layout. Each half is translated alone, so the translation can read badly.

Why: a subject printed over several lines read as separate blocks, or a sentence that runs across a page break or a column.

What to do: join the two blocks (Blocks level) if they are one sentence; leave them if the first really ends there (a heading, a list's lead-in).

A block of several lines, most ending in a comma, read as one paragraph: probably a list (of offices, posts, addresses) whose lines should each stand alone.

Why: the list is outside the copy list, where lines are kept apart.

What to do: split the block at each line (Blocks level), or give it the copy list section if it is one.

A table whose grid is hard to be sure of (merged cells under a header of several rows, many empty cells, a page read by OCR), or text that looks like a table the layout did not find (mostly numbers and percentages).

Why: complex tables are the hardest part of a GR to read; the reasons are in the issue.

What to do: compare the table with the PDF, cell by cell where it matters (amounts, names); fix at the Tables level.
